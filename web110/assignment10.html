During this week's sesssion we took a deeper dive into the skeleton of a website by learning about CSS. While we use HTML to put words in the headings of a page, we use     CSS to make the words look nice. I began by completing the W3Schools CSS Tutorial, and once I noticed the pattern it started to click. You pick what you want to change, like     a heading, and then you list what you want to change about it, like color and size.

   

I tried two ways of adding CSS right inside my assignment page. The first was to put the style directly onto one paragraph. However, that only changes the one paragraph   and nothing else. The second way was to put a block of styles at the top of the page, which makes changes apply to the entire page. I gabe the heading a different color,   background, and font. I enjoyed playing around and seeing which styles I liked better. It was fun to see this happen on my own page instead of just reading about it.

 

I enjoyed putting together a style file for my whole site. I made a file and put in the background color, the font, the spacing, and how I wanted the headings and     and paragraphs to look. All of my pages got the same look at the same time. I can see this week's lesson being useful as I transition into working in at IT or at a help     desk. Typically, companies will have a bunch of internal pages to keep up, such as a company portal and a page that communicates if the systems are up or down. With one     shared style, I could change the look for every page by just editing the one file, and everything stays matching. If a style is not appearing in the way I want, I know to   check if another style is taking over. Overall, my website is becoming more alive, and I am enjoying learning how to play around with its design.
