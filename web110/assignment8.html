Going over the materials from this session showed me that Internet security has multiple layers. In order to protect yourself online you need a combination of strong passwords, frequently updating your software, antivirus, and a firewall that will work together to provide the baseline protections.

The internet safety tutorials covered the basic and frequent threats you can run into online like viruses, malware, worms, and phishing scams. This was the first time I've learned about worms, and I thought it was interesting how they work by basically being able to spread on their own to computers that are connected through the same network. This led into phishing, and being aware of what to look for in scam emails that are trying to steal your information. Some of the warning signs include urgent messaging in emails that uses fear to prompt the user to click on links that compromise your information. A real world application to this knowledge is beneficial because you are aware of what to look out for when you receive suspicious emails. In fact, at my current job we have monthly computer security modules that cover this sort of information specifically to prevent any issues.

The password managers and multi-factor authentication activity went into more depth about setting up strong passwords and keeping track of them. A password manager will store all of your login information in an encrypted vault that opens up with a master password. It can help create and then save strong passwords that are difficult to guess for different websites, so you never have to use the same one more than once. Multi-factor authentication adds another layer of protection because it can send a code to your phone to verify your identity, especially when you are trying to log in from a new device or a location that isn't typically where you are. That way you either just verify it's you and access your account, or you receive an alert that somebody is trying to log into your account. In the real world, companies may require a multi-factor authenticator to be used when accessing sensitive company information. Having a reliable password manager is a nice safeguard tool to use for both professional and personal use in general.

The final activity that I thought was more fun and interesting to look at was to test the firewall with ShieldsUP!. My favorite part about doing these activities is testing out how my home network fares compared to the baseline standards for the various topics we've learned all semester. By doing the ShieldsUP! test the first thing I saw was that it displayed a name for my machine that is tied to my Internet connection. This showed me that you're never truly anonymous online. My test came back with no response which is a good thing because my router ignored outside probes. In real life situations, this is a good regular check-in to do both at work and at home ensuring that your network is protected since default settings may not always be the safest ones.

Overall, through completing the materials from this week I learned that you can use as many tools as you can think of to protect yourself and your data online, but when ever a person is involved there's always room for error and for something to be compromised.
